No Council attempt is consumed by inspection. Financial model or reference gaps may remain; a request for pricing is not an acceptance of a modeled deal.
- Normalize the explicit declared supplier through Atlas. Require supplier role, USD, exact region and scheme-scoped item classifications, and an observation no more than 24 hours old and not in the future. Do not infer buyer ownership, actual capacity, licenses, sanctions clearance, contact identity, quote or commercial suitability. Capacity is included in the normalized declaration fingerprint but not compared with a made-up price.
- RFQ v2 adds the supplier declaration SHA-256 to the immutable draft identity, derived from normalized fields including observed UTC, classification/region sets, exact declared capacity and source URL. Only fingerprint plus existing declared supplier identifier and explicit draft terms are stored; not raw profile name, URL, capacity or contact data. A new declaration creates a different draft and cannot inherit acknowledgment. A fingerprint is not factual evidence.
- Re-resolve archive and supplier around save/acknowledgment, require literal explicit confirmation, reject legacy v1 drafts for scoped acknowledgment while permitting exact historical retrieval. Separate database transactions are not distributed atomicity; a historical record may commit before a subsequent drift error, and no current result is returned then.
- RuntimeGuard exposes only prepare/save/history/acknowledge for a saved plan, on the owning thread, while healthy, unhalted, idle and nonreentrant. No worker or arbitrary agent dispatch; future native GUI must also check its unjournaled worker future. Existing direct local libraries are trusted, not an authentication or sandbox boundary.
